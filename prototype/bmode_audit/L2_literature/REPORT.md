## 1. Q1 -- At which order does the B-mode auto-power first appear, and is there any bispectrum term?

The standard perturbative treatment expands the distortion tensor psi_ij = psi^(1) + psi^(2) + psi^(3A,B,C) + ... in the potential Phi (Krause & Hirata 2010, arXiv:0910.3786, Eq. 7) and classifies power-spectrum terms by the orders of the two fields, "11", "12", "13", "22". The literature statements, verbatim:

* **Krause & Hirata 2010, Sect. 3.1** (grep of ar5iv text): *"The '12' terms are mathematically of order Phi^3, and hence one might expect them to be present if the matter bispectrum is non-zero. However, they vanish in the Limber approximation due to the W(chi',chi) factor in Eq. (18), which is zero whenever chi'=chi."* and, after Eq. 36: *"There is no '13' B-mode shear or rotation power spectrum because gamma_B^(1)(l,z) and omega^(1)(l,z) vanish."* Eq. 30 likewise *"omitted terms such as Delta C^(12)_{gamma_E} which vanish under the Limber approximation."* The leading B-mode is the '22' term, Eq. 31: `Delta C^(22)_{gamma_B}(l) = 4 l^2 int d^2l'/(2pi)^2 (l' sin phi_l')^2 [l'.(l-l')]^2 M(l',|l-l'|)`, with `M` (Eq. 25) a double line-of-sight integral of `P_Phi P_Phi` -- i.e. O(Phi^4), Gaussian. The connected (trispectrum) O(Phi^4) piece exists but *"the connected contribution to the B-mode spectrum is downweighted by the geometric projection factors, especially one-halo and (13) two-halo are strongly suppressed."*
  Reading for the theorem: the B-mode '12' term is `<gamma_B^(1) gamma_B^(2)>`, which is zero identically because `gamma_B^(1)=0` (K&H after Eq. 13: `gamma_B^(1)(l)=0`, `<gamma_E gamma_B>=0`); the Limber argument is only needed for the E-mode '12' term. This is exactly the THEOREM: the term linear in the bispectrum cannot feed `C_BB`.
* **Cooray & Hu 2002, astro-ph/0202411**: B-mode power from the Born correction (Eq. 22) and lens-lens coupling (Eq. 30) is `P^(22) = 4 int d^2l' ... (l'.l'')^2 M(l',l'')`, a convolution of two potential power spectra; *"The first-third order term vanishes under the Limber approximation since lenses at the same redshift cannot lens each other."* Abstract: *"all these corrections are at least two orders of magnitude below the convergence or E-mode power."*
* **Hirata & Seljak 2003, astro-ph/0306354**: the curl `C_l^{omega omega}` arises at second-order Born from the Gaussian (trispectrum) part; `C_B = C_omega` at second order on small scales (used by Becker 2013 and Takahashi 2017 as the numerical check).
* **Shapiro & Cooray 2006, astro-ph/0601226**: *"Third order corrections vanish in the Limber approximation"*; the extra O(Phi^4) term they add affects kappa/E only and *"leave[s] the power spectra of the lensing shear B-mode and rotational component unchanged."* (Pratten & Lewis App. B: those extra terms in fact vanish, "as noted by Krause & Hirata".)
* **Pratten & Lewis 2016, arXiv:1605.05662, Sect. III.2**: *"Since there is no first order field rotation, its power spectrum comes entirely from second-order lens-lens coupling terms"*, Eq. 40 `C_L^{omega omega} = 4 int d^2L'/(2pi)^2 (L x L')^2 (L'.L'')^2 / (L'^4 L''^4) M(L',L'')`; *"the rotation modes are uncorrelated to the leading-order convergence modes, meaning that the cross-spectrum vanishes C_L^{kappa omega}=0, as expected from their different parity"*; App. B: under Limber the only surviving '13' term is 13D (ray deflection) and it has `G_{gamma_B}^{13D}=G_omega^{13D}=0` (Eq. 94).
* **Marozzi, Fanizza, Di Dio & Durrer 2017, arXiv:1612.07263, Sect. 5.1** (CMB polarization at NLO): the terms that *"depend on the reduced bispectrum ... In the Limber approximation given in Eq. (5.20) these contributions vanish due to the Dirac-delta function, delta(r'-r'')."* Their non-negligible NLO B-mode correction is the polarization (Sachs-basis) rotation, fourth order, "comparable to cosmic variance for ell >~ 3500".
* **Bernardeau, Bonvin & Vernizzi 2010, arXiv:0911.2244** (full-sky second order): B-modes and rotation are absent at first order and appear at second order in the field (lens-lens, Born/geodesic, vector and tensor terms); no bispectrum-linear B-mode; no numbers.
* **Lewis & Pratten 2016, arXiv:1608.01263**: rotation-bispectrum contributions to the lensed CMB spectra are *"at most the O(1e-4) level"*; bispectrum corrections to lensed EE/BB ~0.3% -> <=0.15% with non-perturbative smoothing.

**Answer to Q1:** yes -- every perturbative treatment (2002-2017, galaxy and CMB lensing) has the leading B-mode/rotation auto-power at O(Phi^4): the Gaussian `P x P` lens-lens ('22') term plus a geometrically suppressed connected-trispectrum term. There is no O(Phi^3)/bispectrum term in `C_BB` or `C_omega`, for the reason the THEOREM states (`gamma_B^(1)=omega^(1)=0`, hence no '12' and no '13' B-term), and the bispectrum-linear '12' term that does exist for E/kappa vanishes in the equal-shell (Limber) reduction because the lens-lens kernel `W(chi',chi)` is zero at `chi'=chi`.

## 3. Q3 -- Size of the non-Gaussian (beyond Gaussian post-Born) corrections to rotation/B in simulations

* Fabbian, Calabrese & Carbone 2018 (arXiv:1702.03317, DEMNUni multi-plane ray tracing, CMB source): the simulated rotation power *"agree[s] to better than 5% at all scales and to better than 1% on scales around the rotation power spectrum peak"* with the Gaussian post-Born formula using Halofit; kappa variance agrees to ~3%; omega kurtosis 3.2 (the field is non-Gaussian, but its power is Gaussian-predicted).
* Fabbian, Lewis & Beck 2019 (arXiv:1906.08760, galaxy source bins): *"For the bins z>=0.6 ... we obtain agreement at the few-percent level. To our knowledge this represents the most accurate numerical validation of analytic predictions of the rotation power spectrum for weak lensing"*; z=0.2-0.35 degraded to ~20% by ray-tracing resolution; post-Born kappa differs from Born by ~0.05% at z=2.
* Takahashi et al. 2017: rotation at z_s=1.033 "agrees closely" with Krause & Hirata; CMB curl agrees at ell<1000 (vector/tensor contributions negligible there).
* Hilbert et al. 2009: the only sizeable disagreement (factor ~2 on small scales; ray-traced B falls faster than theory on large scales) -- older theory inputs, and in the direction of *less* large-scale B.
* Robertson et al. 2024: non-Gaussian biases to the curl cross-spectrum are Gaussian-only at Simons Observatory sensitivity and ~1 sigma for CMB-S4 quadratic estimators.
* Boehm et al. 2016/2018 (arXiv:1605.01392, 1806.01157): the LSS bispectrum produces an N^(3/2) bias of 0.5-2.5% on the *reconstructed* CMB lensing power (an estimator bias absent in EB,EB), not a curl power.

**Answer to Q3:** beyond-Gaussian corrections to `C_omega ~ C_BB` are at most a few percent (percent-level where resolved), of an already 1e-5-1e-3 signal; no ray-trace shows an O(1) excess, and none shows a large-scale (ell <~ 100) B-mode enhancement.

## 4. Q4 -- Does anyone claim a bispectrum-sourced B-mode at percent-of-E?

No. Searched analytic and simulation literature 1997-2026 (Bernardeau et al. 1997 and Bernardeau 1998 discuss lens-lens and source-clustering corrections to kappa moments only, no B-mode). The percent-of-E B-modes in the literature are all of other origin: source clustering below ~1' (ell >~ 2e4), intrinsic alignments (TATT: linear tidal alignment gives E only, `C2`/`b_TA` give B; Georgiou et al. 2023 forecast S/N ~ 12 for LSST cluster IA B-modes), relativistic frame-dragging/Sachs-basis terms at ell <~ 10 (Magi 2026), and instrumental (Asgari et al. 2019: 2.7 sigma KiDS-450/CFHTLenS, 2.8/5.5 sigma DES-SV, consistent with PSF/repeating additive bias; Daley et al. 2026 UNIONS-3500 [[daleyUNIONS3500WeakLensing2026]]: >4 sigma COSEBI n=1 excess over 1-250', oscillatory at the 6-14' CCD scale, passing after 12-83' and 300<ell<1600 cuts, min PTE 0.18; KiDS-1000 COSEBIs B consistent with zero, arXiv:2007.15633). The single historical percent-level post-Born B-mode claim, Hagstotz, Schaefer & Merkel 2015 (arXiv:1410.8452, "up to 10 per cent on all scales to the B-mode spectrum"), was superseded: Marozzi et al. 2017 Sect. 1 note *"an important cancellation which reduces the final result by more than an order of magnitude has been missed"*, and Pratten & Lewis 2016 give the corrected, negligible values.

## 5. Benchmark of the paper's claim

* Paper/Letter: `f_BB = C_BB^FK / C_EE^O0` = 6.3e-3..8.3e-3 at ell=60 and 3.8e-3..5.0e-3 at ell=1500 (z_s=5); 4.4e-3..5.4e-3 and 2.7e-3..3.3e-3 (z_s=1); "exceeds the sum of every known cosmological B-mode by 8 to 16" (Letter, from K&H's factor 500/3000).
* The K&H sentence is real (Sect. 3.4, lensing-bias paragraph, q<=2, ell<=1e4) but is a worst-case bound saturated at the smallest scales and highest z_s. The multipole-resolved ray-traced post-Born B/E at z_s=1-2 is <1e-5 at ell<~100 (Hilbert) and <=1e-3 at ell~1e3-1e4 (Hilbert, Takahashi), i.e. the paper's FK B-mode is ~300-500x the post-Born expectation at ell=60 and >~3-30x at ell=1500 for z_s=1 -- the Letter's "8-16x" understates the gap by using the bound instead of the value.
* Structurally, the paper's FK B-mode is a term linear in the three-point cumulant, which the literature (Sect. 1) shows cannot contribute to `C_BB` at all; the paper's own earlier version (`C_EE^FK = C_BB^FK`, i.e. `xi_-^FK = 0`) and the 2026-08 audit's spin-channel defects (permutation ambiguity, `zeta_Bmod = zeta_TTT` with the pair phase dropped) are consistent with the B-mode being an artifact of the vertex/transform rather than physics.
* Side note on the FK E-mode: the literature analogue (K&H '12'; Petri, Haiman & May 2017's `2P_{born,ll} + 2P_{born,geo}`, measured as the dominant Born-vs-ray-traced residual of `C_kappa` at z_s=2) exists but vanishes in the equal-shell/Limber reduction the paper uses, and the total post-Born E/kappa corrections for galaxy sources are <~0.05-2% (Fabbian-Lewis-Beck 2019 ~0.05% at z=2; Hilbert 2009 <~2% at ell<1e4); the paper's 1.3-1.7% is at or above the top of that range.

## 6. Practical notes for the simulation agents

* Takahashi 2017 App. C: the omega field at z_s=1.033 is validated against the Gaussian post-Born prediction; the shear B-mode carries numerical E->B leakage that worsens at Nside=4096. Use omega (and the `C_B = C_omega` identity, Hirata & Seljak 2003; Becker 2013: equal to <~1%) as the clean post-Born observable, and expect `C_BB/C_EE <= 1e-3` over ell=2-1e4.
* The zs38 (z_s=5.342) map would give the first galaxy-source B/E at z_s~5; the expected value is bracketed by z_s=2 (Hilbert) and CMB (~1e-4).
* A direct `C_ell^{BB}` from K&H Eq. 31 (pyccl P(k)) at z_s=1 and 5 for ell=60 and 1500 should replace the aperture-mass proxy used in Sect. 5 for the ell=60 factor.
