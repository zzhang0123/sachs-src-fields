## 1. The THEOREM in the literature (perturbative)

| Paper | Statement | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Cooray & Hu 2002, ApJ 574, 19 (astro-ph/0202411) | B-modes arise only from Born correction + lens-lens coupling, "second order in the power spectrum" (O(Phi^4)); "at least two orders of magnitude below the convergence or EE-mode power" | abstract, eqs. 24-32 |
| Hirata & Seljak 2003, PRD 68, 083002 | at second order C_BB = C_omega (rotation) | cited as the identity by Becker 2013, Lepori 2020, Takahashi 2017, Magi 2026 |
| Krause & Hirata 2010, A&A 523, A28 (arXiv:0910.3786) | expansion to O(Phi^4): "Noting gamma_B^(1)(l)=0 and <gamma_E gamma_B>=0" (eq. 29); the "12" terms "are mathematically of order Phi^3, and hence one might expect them to be present if the matter bispectrum is non-zero. However, they vanish in the Limber approximation"; "There is no '13' B-mode shear or rotation power spectrum because gamma_B^(1) and omega^(1) vanish"; only the "22" term <gamma_B^(2) gamma_B^(2)> survives (Table 2); B "suppressed by at least a factor 500 (z<=3), 3000 (z<=1)" including reduced shear and lensing bias | Sect. 3.1, 3.3 |
| Bernardeau, Bonvin & Vernizzi 2010, PRD 81, 083002 (arXiv:0911.2244) | full-sky Sachs equation to second order; the second-order shear "will contain also BB modes"; no cross term with the (pure-E) first order | Sect. 4.1 |
| Pratten & Lewis 2016 (arXiv:1605.05662) | post-Born rotation = curl B at O(Phi^4) for CMB lensing | |
| Magi, Lepori & Adamek 2026, JCAP 08 (2026) 024 (arXiv:2603.24179) | Jacobi map formalism: "image rotation and the shear B-mode ... receive their leading contributions at second order"; "Second-order scalar-induced B-modes and rotation ... from quadratic couplings of the first-order Weyl potential"; C_BB = C_omega at l>>1 with ~5% relativistic corrections at l~5; frame dragging gives a first-order B (no rotation) that dominates at l<~10 but is "percent level relative to the total ellipticity B-mode" | Sect. 3.2, 5.3, 6.2 |

No paper found contains a shear B-mode power term linear in the matter bispectrum. The literature is unanimous that C_BB starts at <gamma_B^(2) gamma_B^(2)> (Gaussian FF-like + connected trispectrum), exactly the operator's THEOREM. Corollary that the Letter itself makes testable: NUMBERS.md line 88 records "rotation vanishes iff C^BB is pure FF; C^BB is 99% FK", i.e. the claimed FK B-mode has no rotation, whereas every full-Jacobian code measures C_BB = C_omega to <=1% (Becker 2013) and the exact Jacobi map integration confirms it up to ~5% at l~5 (Magi 2026).

The claimed FK B-mode is 3-6 orders of magnitude above every simulated post-Born B-mode and above the exact-Sachs measurements, it has no third-order counterpart in any perturbative treatment, and its "no rotation" property contradicts the C_BB = C_omega identity verified to 1%.

## 4. Recommendations

(a) Directly measure the total B-mode and rotation at z_s ~ 1 and ~ 5.
1. Takahashi 2017 nres12 r000 zs16 (z=1.033) and zs38 (z=5.342), already on disk: measure C_omega (pseudo-scalar, clean, converged at l<~1000 vs KH10) and C_BB from the (gamma1, gamma2, omega) fields; use C_BB - C_omega as the leakage diagnostic (Takahashi App. C shows the B leakage at Nside 4096; the paper's damping model l_res = 1.6 Nside applies). If the leakage floor is too high, fetch the same planes at nres13 (12.9 GB, 108 realizations) or nres14 (51.5 GB, r000 only) from sub1/nres13, sub1/nres14. This is the only public full-sky product with omega and shear-with-B at z_s up to 5.3, and it is the strongest end-to-end test: an FK B-mode of f_BB ~ 6e-3 at l=60 would be ~3 orders above the measured post-Born level.
2. RayGal relativistic catalogues (public, exact geodesics with finite beams): Narrow 2 (z<2, 2500 deg2, 3.5e8 particles) and Narrow 1 (z<10, 400 deg2, 1e8 particles) carry a11,a12,a21,a22 per source, so omega = (a21 - a12)/2 and the shear B-mode can be estimated on the cut sky at z_s ~ 1-2 and ~5 (pseudo-C_l or catalogue xi_-/xi_+ with the figure12.py Wigner-d transform); this is independent of the multi-plane approximation and includes ISW/RS, Doppler and potential redshift perturbations (no frame dragging). The public HEALPix maps carry kappa, gamma1, gamma2, 1/mu (no omega) at z=0.7-1.95 (Nside 4096) and 3.0-9.5 (Nside 8192).
3. Cross-check against the multi-plane codes' own quoted levels (Becker 1e-6 at z=1.1; Hilbert <1e-3; Fabbian/Pratten-Lewis for omega).

(b) Shells or potentials to drive our exact Sachs solver.
1. Best physics match: gevolution metric light cones (phi, psi, B_i on HEALPix shells) - our own cb3 run (already validated Phi00 = -A_tr delta to 1-2%, F32) and the UNITY suite by arrangement with P. Bull (53 runs, full sky to z=0.85, 1.75 Mpc/h). These give Phi00 and Psi0 natively without the density-to-driver transfer.
2. Best resolution/depth with the potential itself: RayGal gravity light cone (potential + force on AMR cells, double layer for d/dt; full sky z<0.48, 2500 deg2 to z=2, 400 deg2 to z=10), 1-100 TB, by arrangement with Breton and Rasera; Magrathea's ASCII gravity-grid format documents exactly the fields needed.
3. Best statistics with density shells only (Poisson solve per shell, i.e. the transfer relation validated in wpc_cb3): CosmoGridV1 (69 shells, nside 2048, z<3.5, 2500 cosmologies), Gower Street (~100 shells, NSIDE 2048, 791 wCDM runs, tar.gz per run), FLAMINGO total-mass shells (Nside 16384 to z=3/5, http-range access), AbacusSummit particle light cones (10% subsample, Nside 16384 pixel indices). Takahashi releases no shells, so it can be a target for (a) but not a driver for (b).

(c) Compare an exact-Sachs code's output to ours.
1. snapshot-raytracer (Adamek/Magi, MIT, CUDA): the closest analogue of sachsray (geodesic + geodesic deviation, full sky, validated on C_BB = C_omega at the 1e-15 level); run both on the same gevolution snapshot (cb3 phi/chi -> Weyl potential, B=0) and compare kappa, gamma_E, gamma_B, omega maps and spectra. Needs an NVIDIA GPU (A100/RTX/Grace-Hopper makefile targets); on the M3 Ultra it would need a port, or the comparison can be done by importing their published Fig. 2-3 amplitudes at zs=0.5.
2. Magrathea-Pathfinder (C++/MPI, builds on macOS with HDF5/HEALPix/cfitsio): feed it a gevolution snapshot converted to its 10-column ASCII gravity grid (or the RayGal cells) and compare its finite-beam A (incl. omega) against sachsray's J; also compare against RayGal's public catalogues for the same lines of sight.
3. Multi-plane cross-check on identical shells: CALCLENS (C, public) or DORIAN (Python, public) or LensTools run on the same HEALPix shells we feed to sachsray should reproduce our post-Born omega and B to within the thin-lens error, isolating any FK-like excess as a property of the vertex rather than of the integrator; Hilbert et al. 2020 gives the expected <~2% code-to-code C_kappa scatter.
