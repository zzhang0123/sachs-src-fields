## 1. The premise gamma_B^(1) = 0 in the paper's model (item a)

`sections/cosmology.tex` Eq. (Psi0 scalar): Psi0^(1,s) = -(E0^2(1+z)^2/a^2) Z^i Z^j d_i d_j (Phi+Psi). For a function of chi n, Z^i Z^j d_i d_j f = chi^-2 eth^2 f on the sphere exactly, because Z^i Z^j n_j = 0 and Z^i Z^j d_i n_j = Z.Z/chi = 0. Per multipole this is the paper's sqrt(L^2(L^2-2))/chi^2 = sqrt((l+2)!/(l-2)!)/chi^2 (Appendix E, Eq. appendix screen hessian). A spin-2 field of the form eth^2(real scalar) has B_lm = 0 identically for every realisation. The linear Sachs response gamma^(1) is a lambda-integral of such shells with a spin-0 kernel R (Eq. explicit resp op), so gamma_B^(1) = 0 as a field, not merely <|gamma_B^(1)|^2> = 0. The paper states this itself (conclusion.tex 49-52, F1).

Vector sector (restored 2026-08-29, commit e401b93): Eq. (Psi0 B) = (E0^2(1+z)^2/a^2)[eth^2(n.B) + D eth(Z^j B_j)]. The first term is eth^2 of a scalar (pure E). The second is eth acting on the spin-1 screen projection of a transverse vector; the curl part of Z^j B_j gives a genuine B-mode. So the full Poisson-gauge theory does have gamma_B^(1) != 0. But:
- The paper's FK does not use it: the commit message says "No reported number moves: B_i = 0 in every result, the numerical work being scalar-only"; cosmology.tex: "Vector modes have no growing linear mode in LCDM, so Section insights keeps the scalar sector alone"; Appendix E builds zeta through P_delta -> B_delta -> B_Phi -> (trace, trace-free screen Hessian) of a scalar. Every leg of every channel (TTT, TTP, TPP, PPP, Bmod, Dmod) is eth^2 or nabla^2 of a scalar.
- Magnitude if it were included: LCDM frame-dragging potential is at most 1% of the scalar potential in second-order PT (Lu, Ananda, Clarkson & Maartens 2009) and 0.1-1% of the nonlinear Newtonian potential in N-body (Bruni, Thomas & Wands 2014; Adamek et al. 2016 gevolution: B_i "at least two orders of magnitude" below the scalars). Shear B-power scales as the square, so B/E <~ 1e-4 at the most nonlinear scales and 1e-6 to 1e-8 for ell <~ 1000. Saga, Ichiki, Sugiyama & Yamauchi 2015 (PRD 92, 063533): the second-order-vector B-mode shear is comparable to the r = 0.1 primordial-GW curl at ell ~ 200 and "cannot exceed the expected noise of ongoing and upcoming weak lensing measurements". Thomas, Bruni & Wands 2015 (JCAP 09, 021): vector contribution "negligible with respect to the conventional Newtonian scalar potential, even on non-linear scales". arXiv:2603.24179 (2026): frame dragging dominates the shear B only at ell <~ 10 and is "at the percent level relative to the total ellipticity B-mode", itself bounded by Krause & Hirata 2010 (A&A 523, A28) at B/E <= 3.3e-4 (z<=1), 2.0e-3 (z<=3). The claimed f_BB = 2.7e-3 to 8.3e-3 sits 2-3 orders above all of these, and a vector B would be a Gaussian two-point term, not O(zeta).
- Tensor sector: same status (not in zeta; r-suppressed, <~1e-4 of E at ell ~ 100 for r = 0.1 and falling).

## 3. Deflection, remapping, reduced shear (item c)

conclusion.tex 74-78: FF is "the unperturbed-path analogue of the post-Born lens-lens coupling, here without the ray-deflection (remapping) contribution that a perturbed light ray would supply"; the FK diagram is evaluated on the unperturbed path with the shear itself (path_int.tex, Eq. explicit resp op: the response carries delta(n' - n)). So multiple deflection and reduced shear are excluded by construction. Literature placement of the leading B-mode: fourth order in Phi (Cooray & Hu 2002 lens-lens B ~ 1e-4 of E; Hirata & Seljak 2003 B = rotation at leading order; Krause & Hirata 2010: all O(Phi^4) terms, total B/E <= 3.3e-4 for z<=1 and 2.0e-3 for z<=3, reduced-shear B ~ 1e-4 of E). Bispectrum-linear (O(Phi^3)) corrections do exist in that literature but only in E/kappa: reduced shear (Dodelson, Shapiro & White 2006; Shapiro 2009), lensing bias (Schmidt et al. 2009), post-Born kappa (Pratten & Lewis 2016). No bispectrum-linear B auto-power exists anywhere in it, consistent with the theorem.

## 5. Curved sky, finite separation, screen bases (item e)

Pure E is a property of the field on the sphere and does not depend on which pair is correlated.
